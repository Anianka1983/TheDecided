How to tell if someone is childfree or just not ready yet

By Anna, matchmaker23 September 20266 min read

"I don't want children" and "I don't think I want children, but I'm not really sure" can sound like the same sentence on a first date, especially after a glass of wine and a genuine wish to seem easy to be with. They are not the same sentence. One of them is a decision. The other is a work in progress wearing a decision's clothes.

The short answer A childfree person has made a settled decision not to have children, and can usually tell you when and how they reached it. A fencesitter has not decided either way, and their answer tonight is a snapshot, not a conclusion. The only reliable way to tell them apart is to ask how the answer was formed, not what the answer currently is.

What a fencesitter actually is

Childfree

Someone who has made a settled decision not to have children. Ask them how they got there and you will usually get a story: an age, a moment, a realisation. The decision has a history because it is finished.

Fencesitter

Someone who has not yet decided whether they want children. Not evasive, not commitment-phobic, just genuinely undecided. Ask them how they got there and the honest answer is that they haven't got anywhere yet, because there is nowhere to have got to.

Both can say "I don't want children right now" without lying. The childfree person means it as a permanent statement wearing the present tense. The fencesitter means exactly what the words say: right now.

Why the same sentence means different things

Dating apps ask for a position and give you a checkbox. They do not ask, and cannot ask, how firm that position is. Someone can tick "doesn't want kids" the week after a hard conversation with a partner, and mean it completely today while remaining genuinely open to it changing in three years. The box does not carry a date stamp, and neither person filling it in is being dishonest.

That is more than a quarter of the people you might match with. Not a fringe group, not an edge case. A meaningful share of anyone's dating pool is, right now, a fencesitter, whatever their profile says.

The questions that actually tell you

  • Ask when, not just what. "When did you know?" gets you a story if they're childfree and a shrug or a vague timeline if they're not. The shrug is information.
  • Ask what would change their mind. A childfree person will usually struggle to answer, because nothing much would. A fencesitter can often name it: a different partner, more financial stability, simply getting older.
  • Listen for the tense. "I don't want kids" is settled. "I don't think I want kids" or "I'm not really feeling it right now" is open, however confidently it's said.
  • Don't accept the checkbox as the conversation. A profile answer is a starting point for a question, not the end of one.
  • Notice if the answer moves depending on who's asking. A settled decision holds steady across conversations. An undecided one often shifts a little depending on the company.

The middle column is the one worth slowing down for. It is neither a red flag nor a green light. It is simply true, and treating it as anything more settled than that is where most of the confusion in this kind of dating comes from.

What this means for how you date

A checkbox can tell you the answer. Only a conversation can tell you how firm it is.

If you are childfree yourself and looking for the same in a partner, the practical upshot is that "no kids" on a profile is a filter, not a guarantee. It screens out the people who definitely want children. It does not screen in the people who have actually decided against them, because those two groups currently answer the question the same way.

The distinction matters most at the point where a relationship starts to feel serious. A fencesitter who moves toward wanting children eighteen months in has not changed their mind, because they never had one fixed. A childfree person who tries to talk you into reconsidering has moved, and that is a different and more difficult conversation to have.

Frequently asked questions about fencesitters and childfree dating

What does fencesitter mean in dating?

A fencesitter is someone who has not yet decided whether they want children. It is not indecisiveness as a character flaw, it is an honest description of where they are. On a dating profile they may tick the same box as someone childfree, but the position behind the box is still open.

Is being a fencesitter the same as being childfree?

No. Childfree describes a settled decision not to have children. Fencesitting describes an open question. The practical difference is stability: a childfree person's answer rarely changes, while a fencesitter's answer is, by definition, still being formed.

How long does it take someone to decide if they want children?

There is no fixed timeline. Some people know in their twenties, others take until their late thirties, and a partner, a health scare, or simply time can move the answer in either direction. What matters in dating is not the timeline but whether the person can tell you honestly that they are still deciding.

Should I date someone who is undecided about children?

That depends entirely on your own position. If you are settled and childfree, dating someone undecided means accepting real uncertainty about the relationship's future, not a guarantee either way. Being honest with yourself about how much uncertainty you can live with matters more than the fencesitter's answer on any given night.

Can a fencesitter become childfree?

Yes, regularly. Many people who eventually identify as childfree spent years undecided first. The decision tends to settle rather than appear suddenly, which is exactly why a single conversation on a first date cannot reliably tell you which way someone will land.
